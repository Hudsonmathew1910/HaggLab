# Five places a small business can start using AI this year

Fictional sample blog post · For demonstration only

Most small businesses do not need a big AI project. The best results usually come from one repetitive task that takes hours every week. Here are five common starting points, and what to watch for with each.

## 1. Answering repeat questions
Customers and staff ask the same questions again and again: opening hours, refund rules, leave policy. An assistant that answers only from your own documents, and shows where the answer came from, can take most of these off your team. Watch for: answers that sound confident but are not in your documents. A good assistant says "I don't know" instead.

## 2. Sorting the inbox
An AI tool can read incoming messages, label them by topic and urgency, and draft a reply for a person to approve. Urgent problems, such as damaged orders, get seen first. Watch for: sending AI replies without a human check, especially for refunds and complaints.

## 3. Weekly reports
Many managers spend two or three hours a week copying numbers into a report. A tool can calculate the numbers with code, build the charts and add a short written summary. Watch for: AI writing numbers itself. The numbers should come from a calculation you can check.

## 4. Product search on your website
Visitors often leave when filters do not match how they think. A shopping assistant lets them describe what they want in their own words and shows matching products. Watch for: recommending products that are out of stock or do not exist.

## 5. Demand forecasting
Simple forecasts from past sales can reduce both empty shelves and overstock. Watch for: forecasts shown as one exact number. A useful forecast shows a range and how accurate it was in the past.

## How to start
Pick one task, measure how long it takes today, build a small first version, and measure again after a month. If it does not save time, stop or change it before spending more.
